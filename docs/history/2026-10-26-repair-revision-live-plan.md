## Measurements and decision rules

1. Acquire the unchanged-base original failure and clean control with consistent
   captured-output judgment. Preserve the chosen arguments, cwd, environment and
   fixture; assess their mapping to the public issue independently.
2. Record first-observation and post-edit-diagnosis offering, selection, dispatch,
   complete/unknown coverage and actual delivery. Collection complete is not repair.
3. If a repair is refuted, inspect the next public hypothesis and patch for a
   substantive response to the exception/unobserved branch. A receipt ID or a
   syntactically valid prediction alone is not useful causal revision.
4. For source revision, distinguish prediction matched/contradicted/unverified
   from original-call support. For test addition, require actual added-test
   selection, identical test bytes before/after the named source edit and the
   observed verdict. PASSED_BOTH remains non-discriminating.
5. When source, call and captured failure are unchanged after a certified test
   addition, check historical diagnosis reuse and its explicit provenance. Do
   not count it as a fresh current-snapshot trace or renew finite allowances.
6. Judge current-diff F0010 removal/control preservation, required checks,
   submission, frozen public assessment, isolated acceptance and safety separately.
   A first successful repair can leave the new post-refutation path NOT_EXERCISED.
7. Record cost, tokens, elapsed time, model/tool/edit counts, comparisons, reserved
   versus spent correction budget, remaining forecast and first unmet stage.

Relevant evidence delivered but ignored or followed by another wrong repair
weakens the proposed behavioral benefit. Missing trigger/selection leaves that
mechanism untested. An untested caller equivalence or changed fresh model caller
limits interpretation. Neither success nor unused budget authorizes another run.
